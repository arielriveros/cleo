# Changelog

Versions follow the scheme `1.x.y.z`, where **x** counts breaking changes, **y** counts major features
and **z** counts fixes and refactors. The leading `1.` is a fixed product-line prefix; `package.json`
stores the remaining `x.y.z` because npm requires three-component semver.

Sections are generated by `tools/changelog.mjs` from the description of the pull
request whose merge cut the release. Sections written before that are generated commit lists.

## Unreleased

Animation grows up from one state machine per character. A machine can now be a shared asset with
layers over bone masks, hold static poses, take one-shot actions from a script without any graph
wiring, and fire events that belong to the clip. Skeletons gained sockets, so a sword finally goes
in a hand. Turn-in-place picks the right clip when the camera swings behind.

### Features

- **The flat 2D frame.** A 2D scene now draws through a frame of its own: the background, one sorted and
  batched pass of tilemap layers, sprites and particles, then bloom and the post chain, at a fixed exposure
  of 1 so a pixel shows as it was drawn. None of the 3D frame runs under it any more (shadows, the lighting
  pass, sky, fog, ambient occlusion, reflections, TAA, motion blur, the exposure meter): on a small test
  scene the GPU frame went from 1.9 ms to 0.85 ms and nine full-screen passes went away.
  - *The engine knows.* A scene's dimension is now part of the scene (`Scene.dimension`), written by the
    editor and stamped on every 2D scene a build ships, so the published player draws it the same way.
  - *The 2D look.* Switching a scene to 2D gives it no tone curve and bloom only from what an overdrive
    drives past white. Switching back restores the 3D look unless you retuned it.
  - *2.5D.* **Renderer › 2D Scenes › Pipeline › Full 3D** draws the whole 3D frame under a 2D scene again,
    for one that mixes models and 3D lights in. In the flat frame 3D models are not drawn and lit sprites
    draw unlit, until 2D lights arrive.
- **The eye menu fits the scene.** The viewport's debug-overlay menu now lists only the rows that mean
  something for the scene on screen: a 2D scene drops Skeletons, Animation blend, Navigation mesh, Foliage
  colliders, GI probes and Wind field, and those draw nothing there even if they were left on in a 3D scene.
  A 2D scene on the Full 3D pipeline keeps every row. In 2D, **Collision wireframes** now switches the boxes
  of Character Body 2Ds and Collider 2Ds and **Trigger volumes** those of Area 2Ds, which were under
  **Editor icons**.
- **AI in 2D games.** An AI Controller now drives a Character Body 2D as it drives a 3D Character.
  - *Perception* looks across the picture plane: the cone opens the way the body faces, reaching up and down
    as far as its field of view does, solid tiles and colliders block the line of sight and one-way
    platforms do not, and a controller's **perception layers** pick which 2D bodies it sees (a guard sees
    the player, not its fellow bats).
  - *Steering* moves a walker along X and a flier in XY. `wander` makes a walker pace between walls and
    drops; with obstacle avoidance on, a walker jumps a wall lower than its jump height and stops at a
    drop unless its target is below; a flier's whiskers fan across the picture plane.
  - *Senses* are measured side-on (the horizontal distance, the height, the angle from the way it faces),
    and three new ones work in 2D and 3D: `wallAhead`, `ledgeAhead`, `targetInFront`.
  - *Navigation.* A **Nav Mesh** in **2D** space bakes a platform graph for a walker — where it fits,
    and the walks, jumps, drops and drop-throughs it can make, every jump and drop flown with the game's own
    follower and body step before it is kept — or an air grid for a flier. `path`, `investigate` and
    `patrol` plan on it and the agent jumps where its path says; patrol routes may stand on different
    platforms.
- **Duskwell, a 2D metroidvania example.** A new example project in the gallery, built on the flat 2D frame,
  the 2D physics and the sprite clips from the art in `build/platformer`: a warrior with a three-hit sword
  combo (a press during a swing, or just after it, chains the next), bats that wake as you pass and swoop,
  two rooms the camera slides between, one-way stone platforms, checkpoint lamps that bring you back whole,
  diamonds, and a dash relic that is the only way across a pit. Pixel-perfect at 480 × 270 under five
  parallax backdrops. Generated by `tools/duskwell/build.ts`; see **Duskwell (the 2D metroidvania example)**
  in the docs.
  - *Fliers.* A Character Body 2D's **One-way platforms** switch off lets it pass through them both ways,
    as the bats do (`CharacterBody2DNode.oneWayPlatforms`).
  - *Pits.* A Camera 2D now keeps the last room's bounds while its target is out of every room (falling
    down a pit, jumping past a room's top), instead of following it out of the world; only a far jump,
    a teleport, lets it go.
- **Sprite clips and a sprite state machine.** An animated sprite can carry an **animation set**: a
  character's named clips (idle, run, attack...), each frame shown for its own time, firing events
  (`onAnimationEvent(event, clip)` on the sprite and its parent) and switching hitboxes (an Area 2D of the
  character, resized per frame) on, playing loop, once (`onAnimationFinished`, a follow-on clip) or
  ping-pong, with its own pivot. An optional **state machine** picks the clip on conditions over
  parameters, after an exit time, from one state or any: a sprite in a Character Body 2D reads the body
  every frame (`grounded`, `velocityX/Y`, `speedX`, `onWall`, `wallSliding`, `dashing`, `facing`, and the
  triggers `jumped`, `landed`, `dashed`), and scripts add their own (`setParam`, `fireTrigger`). Scripts
  `play(name)`, and a `SpriteClipName` field picks a clip in the inspector. The **Clips** section shows
  what plays and previews any clip.
- **2D physics and 2D characters.** 2D games get physics of their own (`src/physics2d`), reading
  tilemaps' tiles directly and moving characters the moment a tile is painted or erased.
  - *Tiles.* A tile's **Collision** is now a choice: solid, a **one-way platform** (stood on from above,
    dropped through with down + jump), a 45° or 26° **slope** or a half block (`TileMeta.oneWay`, `slope`).
  - *Sprites ▸ Character Body 2D.* A character's box, standing on its feet, and the 2D twin of a
    Character: it slides along walls, walks up and down slopes and steps, and keeps its sprite facing the
    way it goes. It **walks** or **flies**, runs and jumps with the feel a designer tunes (a jump height and
    the time to reach it, a heavier fall, coyote time, a jump buffer, down + jump through a one-way
    platform), and a **Controller** drives it as it drives a 3D Character: Source: Player reads Move and
    Jump, Source: AI decides. **Driven by ▸ Add Player Controller / Add AI Controller** adds one. A jump
    peaks at the same height at 30, 60 and 144 frames a second.
  - *A game's own moves are its script's.* A dash, a double jump, a wall slide and wall jump, a jump cut:
    written in the body script's `onMove2D(dt, substep)`, which runs every physics substep before the
    engine steers (return `true` to own it), so each move comes out the same at any frame rate. The
    **Character Body 2D** script starter shows a double jump. Duskwell's warrior has the whole set.
  - *Sprites ▸ Collider 2D* is a solid block or one-way platform that carries what stands on it when it
    moves; *Sprites ▸ Area 2D* is a trigger (`onTriggerEnter` / `onTriggerExit`, layers, area-to-area for
    hitboxes, its offset mirrored with the character it rides).
  - *Queries.* `scene.physics2D.raycast(...)` and `overlapsSolid(...)`.
  - A tilemap only 2D bodies walk on turns **3D colliders** off and builds no 3D boxes.
- **Camera 2D, rooms and pixel-perfect pixel art.** **Cameras ▸ Camera 2D** is the 2D game camera: sized in
  art pixels (**pixels per unit**, a **reference resolution** it shows at least, **zoom**), it follows a node
  by name with a dead zone, damping and look-ahead, and shakes on demand (`shake()`).
  - *Pixel perfect.* Every art pixel is the same whole number of screen pixels: the 2D frame is drawn at
    the art resolution through a camera snapped to the pixel grid and scaled up unfiltered, keeping the
    sub-pixel part of the camera's position, so a slow pan glides. Checked on WebGPU and WebGL2.
  - *Rooms.* **Sprites ▸ Room** is one screen of a 2D world. The camera keeps its view inside the room
    its target stands in and slides into the next when the target crosses over; `onRoomEnter` /
    `onRoomExit` tell the target and the room.
  - *Parallax layers.* **Sprites ▸ Parallax Layer** is a backdrop that moves with a share of the camera's
    travel and tiles to fill the view, with a drift of its own.
- **Sprites sized in pixels, pivoted and flipped.** A tileset's **pixels per unit** sizes every sprite drawn
  from it by its tile's pixels (a 135 px character frame at 16 is 8.4 units), so tiles and characters
  share one pixel grid. **Appearance ▸ Pivot** puts a point of the image on the node's position (a
  character on its feet) and **Flip X / Y** mirror it about that point (`Sprite.pivot`, `flipX`, `flipY`).
  Tilesets can also be cut into free-form **regions** instead of a grid (`TilesetConfig.regions`).
  Atlases imported into a 2D scene come in as pixel art: nearest filtering, no mipmaps.
- **Side-view sprite order on tilemaps.** **Tilemap › Sprite order › Side view** (`Tilemap.entitySort`):
  sprites draw over the entity layer's tiles by sorting order and then scene order, never by height, so in
  a platformer a character jumping past another no longer slips behind it. **By Y**, the default, keeps the
  top-down order.
- **Batched sprites.** Sprites that share a tileset, a blend and their sides now go out as one instanced
  draw call instead of one each, in 2D and in 3D, lit or not, and the editor's icons with them.
  - *The same picture.* The draw order is kept: a sprite joins an earlier batch only where drawing it there
    cannot change a pixel (two additive sprites, or two that do not overlap on screen). Measured against
    the per-sprite path on WebGPU and WebGL2: no difference above frame-to-frame noise.
  - *Per sprite, nothing splits a batch.* Tint, opacity, soft fade, overdrive, exposure weight, follow
    light, the animation frame and a lit sprite's fill ride with each sprite. A new program, `sprite.wgsl`,
    draws the unlit ones; `spriteLit.wgsl` reads the same per-sprite values.
  - *Faster.* With 1,000 sprites on screen the 2D pass went from 14 ms to 2 ms of CPU a frame; with 5,000,
    from 47 ms to 9 ms. Sprites wholly off screen are skipped. The Performance panel's **Scene** section shows
    how many sprites were drawn and in how many draws.
- **Sorting order for sprites.** **Appearance > Sorting order** (`Sprite.sortingOrder`, on `SpriteNode` too):
  higher draws later, on top, whatever the distances. In 3D, sprites draw by sorting order first and back to
  front within one; in a 2D scene it offsets the sprite from the tilemap's entity layer, as an emitter's
  sorting order does. 0, the default, leaves the order to depth and is not saved.
- **Area lights.** A new **Area** light is a glowing rectangle or disk: a ceiling panel, a window, a
  softbox, a screen, a round downlight.
  - *Shaded exactly.* Linearly transformed cosines (Heitz et al. 2016) give a specular highlight that is a
    picture of the panel, crisp on a polished floor and stretched on a rough one, and diffuse from the
    panel's exact form factor, clipped at each surface's horizon.
  - *Real units.* **Intensity** is the whole panel's lumens; **Two-sided** emits from both faces, half
    each. Presets: ceiling panel, fluorescent tube, softbox, window fill, disk downlight, TV screen.
  - *Shadows.* A one-sided light casts through a spot shadow map facing its emitting side, a two-sided one
    through a point light's cube.
  - *Everywhere.* Deferred and forward PBR, terrain and the GI Volumes' relight shade it as an area. The
    other shading models, lit particles and sprites, the fog and custom materials shade it as the spot or
    point of the same lumens, so stored custom materials keep compiling.
  - The viewport draws each one's outline and the side it emits from. Player contract 27.
- **GI Volumes without squares in lamp-lit rooms.** A room lit by lamps showed bright and dark cells
  along the probe grid.
  - *What a ray sees is its whole patch.* A capture now averages what glows across each ray's patch of
    view, and its colour, instead of one point of it. A lamp's glowing fixture caught by one ray of one
    probe made that probe tens of times brighter than its neighbours; in the Firing Line warehouse the
    probes far off their neighbours went from 101 to 47 of 880.
  - *Probe inset.* A volume's outer probes can stand in from its faces (**Probe inset**, 0.5 for a new
    volume): a probe on a floor, a wall or a ceiling sees through it.
  - Bakes are format 2; format 1 still loads, and the inspector marks it outdated. A ray count other than
    64 or 256 says what it snapped to. Turning or reshaping a spot or area light now relights the volumes.

- **Rain and snow.** A Weather volume's new **Falling** setting makes rain or snow come down inside it,
  through the particle system with only the parameters changed.
  - *Realistic out of the box.* Rain falls in thin streaks at about 8.5 m/s, slanting with the scene's
    wind; snow flutters down at about 1 m/s, spinning and drifting. The engine draws both with sprites it
    generates itself, so nothing has to be imported. **Rate**, **Drop/Flake Size**, **Fall Speed** and
    **Wind** tune them.
  - *Matte, and glowing against the light.* Drops have no highlight; like a leaf they let the sun through,
    so rain brightens when the sun is behind it. **Trail Min** and **Trail Max** set each streak's length
    at random between them. Particles gain **Translucency** and **Stretch variation** render settings.
  - *Your own sprites.* **Sprite** takes a tileset's atlas and grid, or any texture cut into a grid. Each
    particle picks a random cell, or plays through them.
  - *One system, however many volumes.* One rain and one snow system follow the camera and keep each
    drop only where the volumes ask for it, as thinly as they ask. Overlapping volumes never double it, a
    dry box nested in a wet one carves it out, and it falls into a box from the sky above.
  - *Stops at the roof.* Where a volume keeps **Shelter**, drops and flakes die on whatever is above, so
    none fall indoors.
  - *Raindrops on wet ground.* While rain falls, rings run across the landscape's puddles and drops hit
    the wet ground round them, as strongly as the ground is wet. Sheltered ground stays still, and the
    ground goes quiet once the rain stops.
  - *Presets.* Drizzle, Rain, a new Storm, Light Snow, Snowed In and a new Blizzard set what falls too.
  - *Open to any effect.* The new particle **Weather** module lives by the volumes and the shelter, and a
    **Near fade** render setting fades particles out by the lens.
  - *Renderer.* **Rain & Snow** (`precipitationDensity`) is how much of it is drawn: 1 on Ultra and High,
    0.7 on Medium, 0.4 on Low. Player contract 26.
- **Wind as a vector field.** The Wind node's air now has a speed and a direction at every point, the
  same function on the CPU and the GPU, built the way Ghost of Tsushima built its wind.
  - *Gusts that roll and veer.* One authored wind whose strength rides Perlin noise scrolled along it, so
    gusts sweep across a meadow, and a broader noise that turns it by up to **Veer** degrees from one
    meadow to the next. **Gustiness** says how much of the air the gusts carry. Each plant leans the way
    the air at its root is going, bark and leaves alike, and its shadow with it. Strength 1 is about 7 m/s
    at a gust's crest. The presets set the new knobs; older scenes load with their defaults.
  - *Local wind.* A Force Field's new **Affects wind** makes it part of the air: a whirlwind the grass
    swirls round, a blast that flattens a meadow outward, a corridor the wind funnels through, eddies
    carried downwind, or a shelter that stills a courtyard. **Wind:** presets for each; the first 8 count.
  - *Particles in the same air.* The particle **Wind** module's **Scene wind** rides the field at each
    particle, the wind fields included, without their pushing twice through the Force Fields module.
  - *Read it from a script.* `scene.windAt(position)` and `WindNode.sampleAt` return the air (m/s) the
    plants are bending to, on the same clock. Cloth catches the same gusts as the trees round it.
  - *See it in the viewport.* The eye menu's **Wind field**, or selecting the Wind node or a wind Force
    Field, draws arrows of the air round the camera, coloured from calm blue to gale red and pulsing with
    the gusts, streaks carried by it, and a glyph for every field that moves it.
  - *Force Field outlines follow their shape.* Switching a field between sphere and box redraws its
    outline at once, not on the next selection.

- **GI Volumes.** A new node, **GI Volume**, fills a box with irradiance probes that light what is inside
  with the light bounced off the scene round it.
  - *Relit, not rebaked.* Each probe's view of the static scene is captured once; the sun with its
    shadow, the lights set to Bake, the sky, emissive surfaces and further bounces are put back on it as
    the light changes, so a day and night cycle needs no new capture.
  - *No light through walls.* Probes inside walls, rocks or the ground are found and switched off, and
    each probe's view of the scene keeps it from lighting what is behind a wall.
  - *Everywhere.* Deferred surfaces take it per pixel ahead of the light probes and the sky light;
    forward materials, lit particles and lit sprites at their centre. On WebGL2 and WebGPU alike, at
    every quality preset (half resolution on Medium and Low).
  - *Saved with the project.* **Save bake** stores the capture, deflated, beside the scene; it loads in
    the editor and in a published game without capturing again, and is captured afresh only when the
    geometry round the volume has moved. Moving things (a script's windmill sails, physics props) are
    left out of it. Trees and bushes painted on a landscape are in it, so a canopy shades what is under it.
  - *Reflections that match.* Inside a volume, a light probe's reflection is scaled to the light the
    volume puts on each surface, so a room the volume darkens stops mirroring the brighter light its probe
    captured, and a floor with no probe no longer reflects the open sky.
  - *Debug channels.* **GI** shows the volumes' light on a mid-grey surface, **GI Share** where they
    light the frame.
  - *Probes in the viewport.* A selected GI Volume shows its probes, each lit by the light it holds;
    switched-off probes are red. The debug visibility menu's **GI probes** shows every volume's.
  - *Cheaper on High.* The GI pass runs at half resolution on High as on Medium and Low, brought back up
    along depth and normal edges (about a third of its cost), and not at all when no volume is in view.
    Each probe's on/off state now rides in its light's alpha, so a lookup reads two textures a probe
    instead of three (another tenth off the pass, and off the bounces of a relight).
  - *Additive volumes.* A GI Volume's **Additive** puts its light on top of the light probes, the scene
    ambient and the sky light instead of in their place, for a scene whose ambient is the dark's own faint
    light rather than the open sky's that a room's walls shut out.
  - *The Valley's dungeon.* Thirteen baked, additive volumes, one a room (3,787 probes): the torches' and
    the moon's light bounced off the stone, warm round a fire, over the dungeon's dim ambient. In its place
    the bounce alone, a fifth to a tenth of that ambient away from the fires, left every cell and corner
    black, and the knight's armour with them. `tools/valley/bakeGI.cjs` bakes both scenes (`--scene`).
    A bake whose scene takes longer than ten seconds to settle (WebGL2 compiling its programs on first
    use) still loads within the minute after, the capture started in its place dropped.

- **Lighting that holds together from noon to midnight.** Torches now light the night in the editor
  as they do in the game, and glare no longer floods the screen.
  - *Moonlight in lux.* The Sky Atmosphere's **Moonlight (lux)** replaces the 0-1 share of the sun,
    which put up to 1,000 lux of moon on an edited night. Old scenes keep their share until it is set.
  - *Ambient that fades.* With the Sky Light deleted, the flat ambient still falls at night (a new Sky
    Light's night ambient is 0.0001 of its day). Probe captures keep point and spot shadows, so a probe
    no longer smears torchlight across a valley.
  - *Pre-exposure.* Every light, the sky, emissives and effects are stored multiplied by a power of two
    near the frame's exposure, so moonlit and torch-lit frames keep the precision a sunlit one has in
    half floats. A day frame is unchanged. Histories rescale exactly instead of resetting. Custom
    materials that add their own light opt in with `cleoPreExposure()`.
  - *Physical sky.* With **Physical Units** (new skies), the sky, the sun disc and the ground are lit by
    the scene sun's own lux, so a reflected sun is no brighter than the one in the sky. Its march
    converges: it stops where the air has thinned out and steps densely near the ground, where an even
    march to a thick atmosphere's top (the Valley's is 1,300 km) skipped the air and left the zenith 17
    times too dark, a navy-black sky over sunlit ground. It is calibrated to measured clear-sky light,
    and the clouds keep the balance against the ground they were tuned to.
  - *Metering.* The meter reads the opaque scene before transparents, particles and sprites, so a fire
    or a glint coming into view no longer pumps the exposure. Turning metering on eases from the
    current EV instead of snapping. The present dithers before its 8-bit store.
- **A physical look.**
  - *Bloom.* The new **Physical** mode is the default: no threshold, a **Mix** share (0.04) of the
    blurred frame is mixed in, so glare follows energy (Jimenez 2014). **Threshold** stays available.
    Fireflies at night are gone: the Karis weight uses exposed luma.
  - *Night Vision* (`purkinjeStrength`). Below a few cd/m² colour drains toward a bluish grey and reds
    darken, per pixel on absolute luminance, so moonlight reads as night while a torch pool stays warm.
  - *AgX Punchy.* A new tone mapper: AgX with Blender's Punchy look, more contrast and saturated colour
    kept rich, so a clear sky stays blue where plain AgX turns it steel-grey. Middle grey is held where
    AgX puts it, so switching keeps the exposure. Player contract 21.
  - *Local Exposure.* Exposure fusion gives each region of the frame its own exposure (Shadow Lift,
    Highlight Hold, Local Strength), with an edge-aware upsample so silhouettes keep no halo. On from
    the High tier.
- **Fire that burns on one beat.**
  - *Flicker.* Point and spot lights get **Flicker** and **Flicker Speed**: seeded noise on the
    intensity only, so cached shadow maps stay valid.
  - *Follow Light.* Sprites and particle systems get **Follow Light** and **Follow Amount**: a flame's
    brightness and its embers' brightness and spawn rate swell with the light they follow.
  - *Lit sprites.* Lit sprites take every clustered point and spot light per pixel, so six-way smoke is
    lit by each torch round it.
  - *Draw order.* Additive particles draw after the sprites, so embers stay in front of the smoke.
- **Volumetric Fog volumes.** A new **Volumetric Fog** node is a box of mist, smoke or dust, sized with
  the viewport's face handles.
  - *Medium.* Density, albedo, anisotropy, height falloff, drifting noise, emission and per-source
    scattering, with six presets.
  - *Lighting.* The sun or moon scatters into it through its cascades, and so do every point and spot
    light (through their shadow maps from the High tier) and the sky's fill.
  - *Rendering.* A froxel grid, integrated front to back with energy-conserving steps and blended with
    the last frame's. It runs as fullscreen passes on both WebGL2 and WebGPU. Overlapping volumes add.
  - *Fog Glow.* A particle system's **Fog Glow** lights the fog alone, in the colour and on the
    flicker of the light it follows.
  - The height fog and god rays are unchanged. A scene with no volume runs nothing.
  - Player contract 19.
- **Weather volumes on the landscape and its plants.** The Weather node is now a box, sized with the
  viewport's face handles like a fog volume, and the weather falls only inside it.
  - *Several at once.* Volumes may overlap; the higher **Priority** draws over the lower, so a dry box
    inside a snowy one carves out a dry pocket. Up to 4 near the camera draw a frame.
  - *Wet ground.* The landscape darkens and glosses in rain, and water stands in its low, flat spots
    (**Puddles**).
  - *Snow.* It settles on ground flatter than **Snow Slope**, low ground first, its edge broken up by
    **Snow Breakup**, and is drawn with a chosen **Snow Material**. That material's maps are baked as one
    more layer of the landscape's surface arrays, so it costs no binding. Without one, flat white.
  - *Plants, one at a time.* Foliage takes the weather of the volume each plant stands in, and bows
    under it individually.
  - *Shelter.* A depth map seen from straight above, of every still, solid model round the camera, keeps
    the ground and plants under a roof or an overhang dry and free of snow. It is redrawn only when
    something in it moves. Each volume can turn it off. Renderer setting `weatherShelterEnabled` and
    `weatherShelterResolution`; off on the Low tier.
  - *Old scenes.* A weather node saved before boxes covers the whole scene, as it did. Player contract 20.
- **The Valley at night.**
  - *Sky.* A 2 lux moon, near white, with the blue left to night vision, and a physical sky graded with
    AgX Punchy.
  - *Fire.* Fire lights flicker, and their flames and embers follow them. The bonfire and fire pits
    glow into the fog.
  - *Fog.* Mist over the pond and at the skull cave's mouth, both gathering at night and at dawn, and
    smoke in the dungeon halls.
  - *Probe.* The valley probe gives reflections only.
- **Rough things stay rough at a distance.**
  - *Normal-map specular antialiasing.* A normal map's far mips average its bumps away, and the lost
    spread now goes into the roughness (Toksvig), on meshes and on every landscape surface. Rock across
    a valley is as rough as it is up close. It is part of **Specular antialiasing**.
  - *Split-sum Fresnel.* The probe, sky and screen-space reflections multiplied a Fresnel term onto a
    split sum that already holds one, so rough ground and cliffs seen at grazing angles reflected two to
    three times what they should.
- **Light probes that see shade.** A probe capture draws the sun's shadow round the probe with a map of
  its own, and the forward terrain is shadowed.
- **A probe lights its volume wherever the camera is.** A probe's lighting is nine spherical
  harmonics now, projected from its capture, and every lighting probe in view (up to 16) lights the
  pixels inside its box, the smallest box first. A house looked into through its door is as dark as it is
  from inside, so walking in changes nothing; it used to be lit like the street until the camera crossed
  the threshold. Forward PBR, Blinn-Phong and cel objects take the same light at their centre, and lit
  particles and lit sprites at theirs. Player contract 23.
- **Reflections the same way.** Every probe's GGX-filtered capture is a layer of one octahedral reflection
  atlas, and every reflecting probe in view gives the surfaces in its box their reflections per pixel, the
  smallest box first, wherever the camera is: a polished floor reflects its room from the doorway as from
  inside. **Box Projection** (per probe) puts a room's walls where they are in its floor. The deferred
  pass samples one texture fewer.
- **Relightable, time-sliced probe bakes.** A bake captures the probe's scene once as a G-buffer, a few
  dozen objects a frame, each face culled to what it sees, and a **relight** lights that capture again
  with no geometry drawn but the sun's shadow. One step a frame, one probe at a time, the old bake shown
  until the new is complete. The Valley's time of day relights every 4 degrees of sun: a frame's probe work
  went from 35-46 ms of CPU (a whole capture at once) to under 2 ms, and from about 10 ms of GPU to under 1:
  a probe's lighting is projected from its capture, with no irradiance cube to convolve.
- **Bake on lights, and out-of-date probes.** Point, spot and directional lights have **Bake**, on by
  default: off, a light lights the scene live and no probe sees it. When a light set to bake is moved,
  turned, recoloured or dimmed after the probes were baked, the probes it reaches say they are out of
  date — on the light's and the probe's cards and as a warning badge in the Assets toolbar, with
  **Re-bake**. Player contract 24.
- **A faster prepare.** The landscape's bounds are cached against its heights, not scanned every frame
  (5.6 ms of the Valley's prepare: it is 0.4 ms now). The Performance page splits prepare into its stages.

- **Gas no longer drifts sideways in still air.** Turbulence and disturbance pushed the whole plume one way (it is about one eddy wide), shredding -- which stretches the flow it finds -- grew that lean step after step, and the effect slid off: the same way every time for a seed, the realistic campfire's smoke half a metre in ten seconds. Shredding now stretches the flow's own detail (the velocity less its neighbours' mean) rather than the whole velocity, with its net push taken back out (`fxShred`), and the random forcing only stirs: each step its net push over the domain is taken back out in proportion to each voxel's share (`removeNetForce`; on the GPU three small passes, `fxForcing`, `fxForcingSum` and `fxForcingApply`, summing in a fixed order). And with open boundaries the air flowing in through a side is still air: it used to carry the edge voxel's own velocity in, so any sideways flow fed itself through the box. - **Lit sprites: six-way smoke and surface-lit liquids.** A sprite or animated sprite can be **Lit** (`Sprite.lit`): it takes the scene's light instead of showing its texture as it is -- the key light (sun or moon) with the cascaded shadow it casts on the sprite and the cloud shadow, the ambient and sky fill, and the strongest light near it, as lit particles take them. With the FX Creator's six-way lightmaps (smoke) each light's direction blends the six baked responses, Unity VFX Graph's six-way lighting, so self-shadowing, back-lit glow and the fire's underside light come from the bake and the direction from the scene; with a normal and an ORM map (a liquid's Surface maps) it is shaded as a surface, diffuse plus GGX highlights; with neither, flat. A new program, `spriteLit.wgsl`; unlit sprites draw exactly as before. The Tileset editor has a **Lighting maps** section and the sprite's Appearance a **Lit** toggle.

It now loops by the positive map's coverage and blends its emission as a plain value (`loopBlend` `cover`).

It now runs the CPU film's step (the bake's) with its sweeps in zebra line order, the ordering that held to the CPU's pool count and wet area across spatters, a pool and mercury; volume is kept exactly. `tests/support/gpuFilmTwin.ts` is the GPU step in TypeScript, tested against the CPU film.

The parameters' format 3 puts the gas solver's speeds and forces in SI (m/s, m/s^2, per second, m^2/s; `fxUnits.ts`), so the voxel size is detail only; format-2 saves migrate exactly. The GPU film steps only a window around the liquid (and goes to 2048 cells a side), so a bigger region or volume no longer costs more per frame.

- **One-shots live in the animator graph, played by trigger name.** A layer can hold a character's
  attacks, rolls and reactions as states a script starts with `setTrigger('Roll')`, playing nothing
  itself and naming no state. For that:
  - A state can be entered **directly by a trigger** (`AnimationState.triggers`, **Enter on trigger**,
    a ⚡ tag on the node), from whatever its layer is doing, with its own blend and restart — no

    the current state's own transitions.
  - The graph draws an **Any State** node, and **Can restart itself** lets an Any State transition
    re-enter the state it is in (a second hit replays the flinch).
  - `triggerInfo(trigger)` answers what a trigger plays — its layer, state, clip, rate and real length —
    and `activeTrigger(layer)` which trigger's state a layer is playing.
  - Trigger names are checked: the editor writes the project's triggers to `.cleo/types/project.d.ts`,
    so `setTrigger` and `TriggerName` fields reject a misspelt name in the script and VS Code editors,

    pose it is still fading out of instead of jumping, and re-entering the same clip cross-fades too.
  - Triggers are lowered once the base and every layer have evaluated, so a trigger two layers read

  - The Valley's player and every enemy type play all their one-shots this way: an **Actions** layer

- **Lights keep one intensity; exposure does the rest.** The way Frostbite, Unreal and HDRP treat
  light: a light's lux or lumens never change with the time of day or the room, and auto exposure
  adapts to them as an eye does. The artistic control moved to the exposure:
  - `exposureCompensationCurve` (renderer setting, *Compensation curve* in the Renderer panel) adds
    stops keyed on the metered scene EV100, Unreal's exposure compensation curve. A negative key at the
    dark end keeps a night or a cave darker than a fully adapted eye would show it. The *Day–Night
    Cycle* and *Cave / Dungeon* exposure presets carry one.
  - Materials gained an **Exposure weight** (`setEmissiveExposureWeight`, 0..1, default 1), like
    sprites and particles already had: a glow at 1 is physical; lower values hold a rune or a magic
    bolt closer to one look at every exposure.
  - Light presets and new lights apply their real values as stored, in every scene. They are no longer
    brightened to the scene's exposure (`presetForScene`, `keyIlluminance` and the `SceneLightLevel`
    type are removed; `newLightOptions` takes only the light type).
- **Night and underground now meter correctly.** Three things only showed once lights kept real
  values:
  - The exposure meter's window ended at about EV 6.5, so every darker scene metered the same,
    whatever was in it. It now reaches EV −3.5 (`LOG_LUMINANCE_WINDOW` −22..8, 96 bins).
  - The baked night sky (moonlit air, stars, airglow, the disc) was a fixed 6% of the day sky, and
    its ground tint kept a tenth of noon's sun at midnight. Both lit the night through the sky light.
    The night sky now scales with the atmosphere's `moonlightIntensity` (unchanged at the default
    0.08), and the ground tint's sun term leaves with the sun.
  - God rays ignored the brightness of the light scattering them, so the moon's haze was the sun's.
    They now scale with the key light's intensity; daylight is unchanged.
- **Cheaper local lights: line-of-sight culling, per-face shadow caching, no wasted shadow taps.**
  - **Light Occlusion** (`lightOcclusionCulling`, on by default, Renderer panel under GPU Occlusion
    Queries): the occlusion queries also test each point and spot light's whole reach against what
    was drawn. A light whose reach is entirely hidden (the torch in the next room) is left out of
    the light grid, and goes to the back of the queue for shadow maps. `renderer.occludedLights`
    counts them.
  - Point shadows cache each cube face on its own, and keep a static-only copy of every face: a face
    with something moving in it (the player, an enemy, a falling barrel) starts from that copy and
    draws only what moves, instead of redrawing all six faces over every wall in range. Spot maps
    are cached the same way, and are now culled to the view and handed out nearest first like the
    point maps. A skinned caster animating in place now updates its shadow (it kept a stale pose).
  - The lighting loops test a light's range and cone before sampling its shadow map, rather than
    running the 9-tap filter for every shadowed light listed in a pixel's cluster.

- **Parallax occlusion mapping is back, with curved silhouettes and depth.** Every PBR material with
  a height map marches it — there is no on/off flag any more, and materials saved with
  `parallax: false` march too. The ray now bends with the surface's curvature, measured per pixel from
  the interpolated normal (Oliveira & Policarpo's curved relief mapping, with no vertex attribute or
  import step), and a ray that leaves the relief layer is discarded, so a sphere's outline is bitten
  into by the relief. The march also writes the hit's depth, so other geometry intersects the bumps
  rather than the polygon. Only the parallax shader variants write depth — the editor now binds them
  too — so materials without a height map keep early-Z. **Clip at UV border** stays for flat faces.

- **Animation Controller assets (`.controller`).** A state machine — parameters, states,
  transitions, layers, action slots — as an asset any number of characters share, like Unity's
  Animator Controller. Create one from **+ Add**, or with **Save as Controller…** in the state
  machine editor; double-click one to edit it on a character built on its rig. A character's
  **Animation** section picks its own machine or a controller, and **Clip overrides (this character
  only)** swaps clips by name. **Apply to Controller** updates every character using it; **Detach**
  turns it back into the character's own machine. Characters hold a resolved copy, so scenes,
  templates, bundles and published games play without the library.
- **Layers and bone masks (`.mask`).** A machine can stack layers over the base — each its own
  states over the shared parameters, with Override or Additive blending, a weight (fixed or from a
  parameter) and a bone mask. An empty state on a layer means "add nothing" and fades the layer out.
  Additive adds the clip's motion relative to its first frame. Only the base layer drives root
  motion. Masks are edited in the rig editor's **Bone Masks** section, with Upper body / Lower body /
  arm / head presets found by bone names, branch-or-single-bone ticking and per-bone weights.
- **Pose assets (`.pose`).** Named static poses, as per-bone deltas from rest. Saving a pose in the
  clip editor now writes into the rig's pose asset; the rig editor's **Poses** section manages them.
  A pose can still ride on a clip as a pose-offset edit, and a state can now hold one (**Plays: Pose**)
  — on a masked layer, a hand grip over locomotion. Poses stored on a rig by older projects keep
  working, and **Move into a pose asset** migrates them without breaking a clip reference.
- **Action slots.** `animator.playAction(clip, { slot, blendIn, blendOut, speed, loop })` plays a
  one-shot over whatever the machine is doing and returns a handle with a `finished` promise;
  `stopAction` and `isActionPlaying` go with it. Slots are declared on the machine with a layer to sit
  above, a mask and a blend mode; with none, an action plays full-body over everything. Also new on
  the animator: `setLayerWeight`, `layerStateName`, `layerNames`.
- **Events on the clip.** A clip carries its own event markers, which fire wherever it plays — a
  state, a layer or an action slot — through `onAnimationEvent`. Authored on the clip editor's
  timeline: **+ Event at playhead**, drag to move, click to rename, double-click to delete. The state
  machine's own event list still works.
- **Sockets.** Rigs carry named attach points on bones, with a position / rotation / scale offset,
  authored in the rig editor with a viewport gizmo and an optional preview model shown in the
  animation editors. They are embedded in every model on the rig, so they ship. Any node follows one
  from its **Attach to socket** section or with `node.attachToSocket('parent' | character, name)`;
  `modelNode.getSocketWorldMatrix(name)` and `animator.getJointModelMatrix(joint)` read them. The
  socket ignores its bone's scale, so an FBX armature's 0.01 does not shrink a held sword, and the
  follow runs after animation and before camera rigs, so nothing trails the hand by a frame.

- **A character can play another skeleton's animation set.** The inspector's **Skeleton** slot now
  lists only the character's own skeleton and the skeletons it has been retargeted to. **Retarget
  skeleton…** picks another skeleton that has clips, shows the bone mapping (**Map bones…**), and **Use
  skeleton** saves it and switches the character to that skeleton's clips, animators and blend spaces.
  The mapping corrections are stored on the character's own rig (`RigAsset.retargetTargets` lists the
  skeletons it is set up for; `ModelAsset.nativeRigId` names its own while it plays another). Sockets,

### Fixes & refactors

- **Opening a scene no longer tilts its 2D view.** The editor remembered the previous scene's view poses
  for the fresh camera an opened scene brings, so switching from one 2D scene to another could come up with
  the 3D camera's angle in the 2D view.
- **Particles over a tilemap draw once.** Under a perspective camera a scene with a visible tilemap drew its
  particles twice (in their own pass and in the 2D pass); under an orthographic one with every tilemap
  hidden it drew them not at all.
- **Existing 2D scenes look different.** They now draw through the flat 2D frame at an exposure of 1
  instead of the 3D frame's metered or authored exposure, through the tone curve they were saved with.
  Switch the scene's dimension to 3D and back to give it the 2D look, or set **Renderer › 2D Scenes ›
  Pipeline** to **Full 3D** to keep the old picture.
- **A sprite at opacity 0 is invisible.** One built or loaded with opacity 0 drew fully opaque until the
  slider was touched: the material it drew with stored `opacity || 1`. The batched pass reads
  `Sprite.opacity` itself.
- **Far tree shadows.** An impostor now casts only in the cascades past its hand-off, where the camera sees
  impostors too; nearer, the coarsest mesh level casts, so mesh trees are no longer shadowed by a round
  blob of their own impostor. Its shadow and its depth take the crown's size, not the impostor's wider
  bounds, and the shadow blends the views round the sun instead of snapping between them.
- **The Valley's sun turns smoothly.** It was stepped every half a degree, so its shadows jumped; each part
  of the renderer already keeps its own threshold for what the sun's motion re-bakes.
- **The Valley's puddle decals are gone:** the weather volume's puddles fill the ground's own hollows.
- **The Valley's houses are furnished.** Beds, tables, shelves, cabinets and a workshop corner (a grindstone
  and bellows) in the Long House and the Old House, 19 pieces from 10 new models, placed against the walls
  clear of the doors and of the Long House's aisle; a rug lies in the patch the afternoon sun lays before
  its side door, so the GI bounces its colours. Eleven candles on the tables and cabinets (60 lm each, no
  shadows) light both houses at night. The Hamlet and both interior GI bakes were taken again with them.
- **Clouds and gusts no longer race when the wind changes.** The clouds' drift and the gusts' travel are
  integrated frame by frame, instead of a speed times a clock minutes along, which raced them across the
  sky whenever a preset changed the wind's speed. The cloud shadows now stay under baked clouds whatever
  the wind does after the bake.
- **`Game.updateRenderSettings` keeps the bloom mode and the Purkinje strength.** A patch reset both to
  their defaults.
- **One math module.** The engine's math helpers now live in `src/core/math.ts` and reach scripts as
  `MathUtils`: scalar helpers and finite guards, damping, angles, seeded random and noise generators, the

  re-export what they used to export.
- **Fixed: a camera cut smeared its first frame with motion blur.** The blur reprojected the new view
  through the previous camera; it now starts fresh after any cut (`renderer.cut()` declares one).

- **Fixed: changing a tileset's tile size dropped its lighting maps from the project's references.**
  The re-slice kept only the atlas in `textureIds`, so a normal or six-way map used only by a tileset
  stopped persisting and shipping with it. The asset reference graph also never listed a tileset's
  lighting maps, so they read as unreferenced and safe to delete.
- **Fixed: a tileset tab restored on reload showed "no atlas image"** until it was reopened: the atlas
  view looked for the texture before the texture store had registered it.
- **Texture slots** (the material editor's, now shared with the tileset editor) draw their texture list
  over the panel instead of inside the section, which could cut it off, close it on Esc, and upload an
  image file dropped on the slot. The engine's `Null` placeholder is no longer offered.
- **Fixed: a scene was marked unsaved on its own.** Anything a node's per-frame update or the physics
  write-back changed (a decorative script turning a windmill's sails) reached the editor as an edit.
  Those writes are now counted as the scene ticking (`authoring.derived`), not as authoring, and setting
  `visible` to the value it already has no longer reports a change.
- **Fixed: bones with exporter numbers did not match.** Sketchfab numbers every node in its glTF
  exports (`mixamorig:Hips_01`), so those rigs matched nothing on retarget, implicit hand sockets or bone
  masks. A trailing `_NN` is now ignored under a namespace. A bare `spine_01` is still its own bone,
  because it is Unreal's real name. Bone masks and pose states also accept another exporter's spelling
  of a bone.
- **Fixed: retargeting from a root hips onto a child hips laid the character down.** When one skeleton's
  hips is its root joint and the other's sits under a parent joint (Sketchfab's `_rootJoint`), the
  correction compared bones posed under different parent frames. It now bridges the two parent frames.
- **Fixed: retargeting between rigs bound in different poses folded the arms into the chest.** The
  correction re-applied the source's motion relative to the target's own bind, so an arm a clip lowers
  90° from a T-pose was lowered 90° from an A-pose too. The target's limbs are now first turned to the
  source's bind pose (`retargetPose`, the "retarget pose" of other engines), so the arm ends up where the

- **The Day–Night exposure preset is darker by day, and darkens toward the sky.** Its compensation
  curve kept rising through daylight, giving back the stop the meter took when the view turned to the

  keeps falling as the scene meters brighter.
- **Fixed: an action's blend-out was shorter the faster it played.** The fade at the end of a

  0.3 s rather than about 0.19 s.
- **Fixed: importing a project left every texture loose in the Assets root.** The texture records are
  derived a render after the explorer starts pruning, so each texture's entry was pruned and then

  clip events and socket attachments. A player built before this ignores all of them without a word,

- **Turn-in-place waits for the aim to settle.** A camera swept round to the back crosses 90 degrees
  long before it reaches 180, so the 90 clip always won. The new Character tuning **Settle**

- **Posing a bone no longer explodes on zero or tiny scales.** The clip editor's bone panel and gizmo

## v1.1.2.4 — 2026-09-01

The relief release. A height map is no longer a per-pixel illusion: on WebGPU it becomes real
geometry in a compute pass, with a real silhouette and real self-shadowing, and the parallax march
that stood in for it is withdrawn. Relief depth gained a unit, so one material reads the same on a
cube, on tiled ground and on a photogrammetry scan. Model import stopped throwing away the maps and
the shared vertices that the .fbx/.glb route was silently losing, and three long-standing leaks are
closed. The editor also left webpack for Vite.

### Features

- **Tessellation and displacement in a compute pass.** WebGPU has no tessellation stage and is not
  getting one, so the tessellator is a compute shader whose output buffers feed the render pipeline
  directly — picked up by every draw path for free, shadows and depth included. Topology is implicit
  (an invocation turns its own id into a barycentric grid position), and it is watertight with no
  deduplication, because two triangles sharing an edge generate the same samples along it. Level 3
  on a 3941-triangle mesh is 177k vertices and 252k triangles for 9.9 MB; the level lives on the
  MATERIAL, beside the map it displaces by, and the inspector reads out triangles, megabytes and
  texels per edge. Skinned models are refused — an animated mesh is deformed by bone matrices in the
  vertex stage, so a dispatch over its buffer would displace the bind pose. WebGL 2 has no compute
  stage and draws the mesh as authored.
- **Relief depth has a unit.** `displacementScale` was a fraction of one texture repeat, which means
  nothing until you know what a repeat is worth: a tiling material puts one inside a few centimetres,
  an atlas-mapped scan puts one around the whole object. Measured on a scanned branch, one UV unit
  was 47.97 world units — so the 0.05 default asked for 2.4 units of relief on a branch 12.7 units
  thick. New materials author depth in **world units**; a material saved before this existed keeps
  UV, since converting the stored number needs the mesh's chart scale and one material can sit on a
  cube and on a scan. The Material inspector now shows what one UV unit is worth on the mesh in
  front of you, and converts the number when you switch the unit.
- **Parallax occlusion is off by default, including for materials saved before the flag existed.**
  Not re-defaulted — **withdrawn**: displacement replaced it for the case it was being used for, the
  editor offers no control for it any more, and a legacy material that kept marching would have had
  no way to stop. The march survives in both PBR chunks and still honours the flag, so an asset that
  explicitly stores `parallax: true` re-enables it.
- **Import recovers the texture maps the glTF2 conversion drops.** The .fbx/.glb route converts to
  glTF2 first, and that exporter writes `baseColorTexture` and essentially nothing else: FBX's
  `Bump` channel arrives as `aiTextureType_HEIGHT`, which it does not look for, and it has no
  destination at all for occlusion or roughness. Those maps are now read back from the same scene
  and merged by material index, never over a slot the glTF itself filled. Any claimed image the
  import still did not take is registered as a texture, instead of vanishing into neither the
  material nor the library.
- **Import welds the mesh that conversion unindexes.** Assimp's exporter hands back every triangle
  owning its own three vertices — 1963 authored positions became 11823 on a scanned branch — so
  nothing interpolates across an edge and both the lighting and the tangent frame go faceted
  (normals at one position disagreed by 55.8 degrees median). Imports now weld on position with a
  45 degree auto-smooth angle, keeping genuine creases hard and UV seams split.

### Fixes & refactors

- **The parallax frame is exact for a skewed UV chart.** It used to force the bitangent
  perpendicular to the tangent, and a chart is under no obligation to be square: 31% of a scanned
  mesh is more than 10 degrees off. Forcing it does not shrink the offset, it rotates it by an
  amount that depends on where the camera sits — 0.4 degrees of error at one azimuth and 28.7 at
  another, on the same surface at the same view angle, which is why zooming looked right and
  orbiting did not. The reciprocal-basis form is exact in every case and reduces to the old frame on
  a square chart, so cubes, planes and spheres are unchanged. Grazing views now shorten the ray
  rather than let the step grow past two texels, which was the smear at 70-80 degrees.
- **Rigid bodies have a centre of mass.** cannon has none — `body.position` IS the centre of mass,
  and shape offsets move only the collision geometry — so a collider fitted to a mesh's bounding
  box left the mass at the node origin with the geometry hanging off it. Gravity applied no torque;
  the ground's normal force did, so bodies rotated until the origin-to-collider vector lined up with
  the contact normal. Everything "stood up" and tilted to match the terrain it landed on. Colliders
  are now re-based onto their volume-weighted centre, and the inertia tensor is measured with the
  author-time rotation taken out.
- **Play sessions no longer leak.** Emptying the physics world spliced while iterating and then
  assigned `world.bodies = []`, which dispatches no events — so half of every session's bodies
  stayed in `SAPBroadphase`'s own list for the lifetime of the page, still sorted, paired and
  collided against. `world.bodies.length` reads 0 either way, so the Performance panel gained a
  `broadphase` row that shows what will actually be iterated. `Scene.dispose()` is the other half:
  nothing released a scene that was thrown away, so each Play leaked its heightfield body, its
  foliage collider pool, its chunk meshes and every model mesh in the tree, plus a permanent event
  subscription.
- **The editor's viewport camera is pinned.** `activeCamera` returned whichever active camera came
  first in tree order, so a game camera loaded from a saved scene outranked the editor's own: the
  viewport rendered through it and the free-fly navigation dragged it around. Authored cameras are
  inert while editing again.
- **The editor builds with Vite.** webpack, Babel and their loaders are gone. Two consequences worth
  knowing: `npm run build` is a real production build (it was a development build, so the deployed
  editor shipped a 35 MB bundle), and `cleo` resolves to the engine source, so an engine edit is
  live with no dist rebuild. Import workers, Monaco and the published player were re-wired to match;
  the player deliberately stays a classic script, because a published desktop game runs from
  `file://` where a module script is blocked.
- Model import stopped discarding the glTF's authored tangents and their handedness — it passed an
  empty bitangent array, which made `Geometry` recompute the whole frame.

## v1.1.0.0 — 2026-08-30

The WebGPU release. The renderer now runs on a hardware abstraction with two complete backends, and
the editor can drive its viewport on either. Lighting moved to real photometric units on a physical
BRDF, and the model pipeline gained LOD generation and a storage format that survives large projects.

### Breaking changes

- Lights are authored in photometric units: lux for directional, lumens for point and spot, with a
  real `range` that also serves as the culling radius. Old scenes, published bundles and examples
  migrate on load, but **a scene saved by 1.1 cannot be read by 1.0**. Migrated lights carry a
  `legacyFalloff` marker and deliberately large numbers; the light inspector says so and offers a
  reset to physical defaults.
- `light.ambient` is gone. Ambient is now `Scene.ambientLight`, in lux, with a colour and intensity
  control in Scene Settings. Recovered from old saves automatically.
- Published games must be republished: `PLAYER_CONTRACT` is now **6**. A skinned model's
  `jointIndices` / `jointWeights` moved out of the manifest JSON into `game.bin` blob chunks. A v5
  player loading a v6 bundle gets skinned characters with no bone weights; `publishClient` refuses the
  mismatch rather than shipping it.
- The model library is stored one record per model with geometry as typed arrays, instead of a single
  array rewritten on every edit. Projects migrate forward automatically, one asset at a time — **a
  project opened in 1.1 will not open in 1.0**.
- `Renderer.screenshot()`, `screenshotOffscreen()` and `renderProbePreview()` return
  `Promise<string>`; WebGPU readback is asynchronous. `screenshot()` is deprecated in favour of
  `screenshotOffscreen()`.
- `Mesh.draw()`, `drawRange()` and `drawInstanced()` throw by name on a non-WebGL2 device instead of
  dereferencing an undefined context. Direct `mesh.draw()` calls are WebGL2-only by contract.
- The G-buffer normal target is octahedral-packed — `rg` normal, `b` reflectance, `a` roughness.
  Screen-space custom materials that read it directly need updating; deferred custom materials fill a
  `Surface` struct and are unaffected.
- A deferred custom material is now skipped, with one warning, in forward passes and light-probe
  capture. It previously drew silently wrong.
- Engine shaders are authored in WGSL; the `shaders/deferred/`, `shaders/environment/` and
  `shaders/ibl/` GLSL trees are deleted and GLSL ES 300 is generated at build time. Forks or patches
  against those paths will not apply.
- Bloom works for the first time — the compose target carried a mip chain nothing generated. Defaults
  were re-tuned with it (threshold 1.0 → 2.0, intensity 0.6 → 0.35), so existing scenes will look
  different.
- Terrain relief is switched off (`TERRAIN_RELIEF_ENABLED = false`) and terrain height authoring is
  hidden. Deferred rather than abandoned; parallax occlusion on ordinary materials is unaffected.
- Lights beyond `MAX_POINT_LIGHTS` / `MAX_SPOTLIGHTS` are excluded rather than running off the end of
  the uniform array.
- `npm run build` now also emits `.d.ts` declarations, and `npm run naga:build` regenerates the
  vendored WGSL translator.

### Features

- **WebGPU backend.** A render hardware interface with two complete implementations — WebGL 2 and
  WebGPU — behind one device abstraction. The Renderer panel has a backend picker; the choice is a
  machine-local preference applied on reload. The editor viewport draws on either backend. Published
  games still run on WebGL 2.
- **Custom materials run on WebGPU** in all three modes — forward, deferred and screen. Your GLSL is
  translated by a vendored `naga` build, and the material inspector reports the verdict, quoting
  naga's own diagnostic when a shader cannot cross over. New `screenUV()` helper for screen-space
  materials, with (0,0) bottom-left on both backends.
- **Photometric lights** — lux and lumens, windowed inverse-square falloff, and `sourceRadius` /
  `angularRadius` for area-like highlights and sun-size-scaled shadow softness.
- **A physical BRDF** — height-correlated Smith visibility, Burley diffuse, per-material reflectance,
  and Kulla-Conty multi-scatter compensation so rough metals stop reading dull.
- **Auto-exposure.** The camera meters the frame and drives exposure itself, with EV min/max clamps,
  compensation in stops, and separate adapt-up and adapt-down rates. On by default.
- **Specular occlusion, horizon occlusion and specular anti-aliasing** — reflections survive in
  corners, the sky-rim on normal-mapped surfaces is gone, and sharp highlights stop flickering.
- **Sky light.** A scene singleton that projects the sky — a baked atmosphere or a skybox — into nine
  L2 spherical-harmonic coefficients, so a surface facing up receives the zenith and one facing a
  sunset receives the sunset. Cloud cover grades it.
- **Parallax occlusion mapping** on PBR forward and deferred paths, with the height map promoted to
  its own texture slot on every material type.
- **Alpha mask and cutout shadows** — `maskMap` and `alphaCutoff` on all three shading models, and
  four new depth programs so a masked surface casts the silhouette it actually draws.
- **Emissive HDR**, including glTF `KHR_materials_emissive_strength`, plus terrain ambient occlusion
  and a final-image saturation grade.
- **LOD generation** in the model editor: an in-house quadric-error decimator with half-resolution
  texture twins shared between levels of the same size.
- **Sub-mesh grouping on import.** Separate and Merge stopped being mutually exclusive — enable both
  to partition an import's parts, each group becoming one asset with its members merged.
- **Foliage at scale.** A density falloff that thins instance counts by distance, a 4M-triangle draw
  budget that stops a heavy prototype removing the GPU device, and a hysteresis band plus amortised
  cell admission so moving the camera no longer spikes. Layers are keyed by a stable id, so renaming
  one no longer orphans its instances.
- **The Renderer panel split in two** — a Performance panel with per-pass CPU *and* GPU columns,
  frame-budget lines and per-pass kill switches that measure marginal cost, and a Renderer Settings
  panel. GPU timers now work on both backends, and a new CPU profiler times the same scopes.
- **Renderer settings persist.** Every control now marks the scene changed, so a tuned look survives a
  refresh — and reaches publish and export, which carry the same snapshot.
- Terrain material previews render on a real patch of terrain scaled to a reference landscape rather
  than on a sphere, and the Add palette gains a Plane primitive.

### Fixes & refactors

- **Cross-backend correctness.** Zero-to-one clip depth on WebGPU; the screen-space Y flip folded into
  the reconstruction and shadow-lookup matrices; mip chains, cube-face bakes and probe captures
  generated the right way up; channel packing no longer vertically mirrored, which had been reading
  authored metallic/roughness maps upside down.
- **Custom materials.** A material whose source never read a declared uniform silently failed to draw;
  a uniform name declared in two blocks is now written to both, which had left forward custom
  materials rasterising nothing at all with the right draw count and no error.
- **Render passes and resources.** Every colour attachment is cleared, not just the first; the forward
  clear is a real pass; depth textures are declared as depth textures and bound only where sampled;
  texture upload address modes are honoured; each draw gets its own uniform slot; WebGL2 pipelines are
  keyed by vertex layout.
- **Instancing.** Each instanced group gets its own buffer, so a second group in a pass no longer
  reads another's matrices or references a destroyed buffer.
- **Shader validity.** Four shaders sampled textures from non-uniform control flow, which WGSL
  rejects — and one rejected module cost the terrain, the clouds and the selection outline all of
  their pixels.
- **SSAO** read the G-buffer normal through a linear sampler, so every normal was a bilinear blend;
  switching SSAO off left the previous frame's occlusion bound, so the kill switch stopped the work
  and left the effect.
- **Auto-exposure** readback placement was causing context loss, and its adaptation speeds carried
  Unreal's names with the meaning inverted, adapting nine times slower than the numbers implied.
- Empty and degenerate draws are skipped rather than recorded; the Performance panel's Physics and
  Scene rows read a field that did not exist and always showed 0.00 ms.
- Internally: comment volume across `src/` and `editor/src/` cut ~37%, `EngineContext.tsx` split into
  five hooks and four modules, and 54 new unit-test files added.
