# Duskwell (the 2D metroidvania example)

A small 2D metroidvania, and the example the 2D tools are built against: one tilemap world split into rooms,
a warrior with a three-hit sword combo, bats, checkpoints, diamonds, and a dash relic that opens the way on.
Open it from the example gallery (**Duskwell**) and press Play.

It is generated by `tools/duskwell/build.ts` from the art in `build/platformer` (see
`tools/duskwell/README.md`); its scripts are `examples/scripts/Dusk*.ts`.

## Playing

| Action | Keyboard | Gamepad |
|---|---|---|
| Move | A / D or the arrows | left stick or d-pad |
| Jump (hold for higher) | Space or K | A |
| Attack (again during a swing, or just after it, for the next of three) | J or left mouse | X |
| Dash (once found) | Left Shift or L | RB or B |
| Drop through a platform | Down + Jump | Down + A |

**The Gate** is where you wake: a step to hop, stone platforms you can jump up through and stand on, and four
diamonds. The lamp by the east door is a **checkpoint**: walk past it to light it, and you come back there,
whole, when you die. **The Long Hall** beyond has two bats, a pillar to climb, more diamonds, a second
lamp, and a pit six cells wide that a running jump does not clear. The glowing relic before the pit is the
**dash**: jump, then dash in the air, to cross.

A bat hangs asleep until you come close, then wakes, flies at you and swoops. Its bite costs one of your
five hearts and knocks you back, with a moment of invulnerability after; three sword hits kill it, and it
drops a diamond. Falling into the pit costs your whole health.

## How it is made

Everything in the scene is an ordinary node you can select and change:

- **Map** — one tilemap, 16 pixels a cell, cells sized one world unit. Its tileset marks the rock tiles
  **solid** and the stone slabs **one-way**; its **3D colliders** are off and its **sprite order** is
  **side view**. See [Tilemaps and 2D](tilemap-2d.md).
- **Rooms** — two **Room** nodes over the map. The camera keeps its view inside the room the player is in and
  slides across when the player walks through the door.
- **Camera** — a **Camera 2D** following `Player`, showing at least 480 × 270 art pixels, pixel-perfect,
  with a dead zone, look-ahead and a shake the player's script asks for on a hit.
- **Backdrop** — five **Parallax Layers**, from a sky that stays on screen to near woods that move with most
  of the camera's travel.
- **Player** — a **Character Body 2D** with the platformer controller. Its **Sprite** carries the warrior's
  animation set (idle, run, jump, fall, three attacks, hurt, death) and a state machine that picks the
  movement clips from the body on its own; its **Sword** is an Area 2D the attack frames switch on and size.
  The `DuskPlayer` script adds the combo, health, the hit, death and the respawn.
- **Enemies** — each bat is a Character Body 2D with no controller and no gravity that passes through one-way
  platforms, moved by `DuskBat`. Its **Bite** is an Area 2D the player's script reads `contactDamage` from.
- **Pickups** and **Checkpoints** — Area 2Ds with `DuskPickup` (its `kind`: `diamond`, `heart`, `dash`,
  `doubleJump` or `wallJump`) and `DuskCheckpoint`.
- **HUD** — a UI root with three texts that `DuskHud` writes each frame.

The 2D scene draws through the flat 2D frame: no 3D passes, a fixed exposure, bloom only from the torches and
diamonds the tilesets' **overdrive** pushes past white.
